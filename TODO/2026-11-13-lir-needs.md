# What Lir needs of Dannan and Dagda (2026-11-13)

Tom, 2026-11-13, to the Lir session: "You all need to discuss your needs and
ensure the the more fundamental projects, namely Dannan and Dagda, support
Macha, Lugh and Lir as first class citizens. This was the purpose of splitting
out the technologies." And, the same day: "It needs to support Macha, Lir, and
Lugh and not as afterthoughts."

This is Lir's side of that discussion, for the Dannan, Dagda, Lugh and Macha
sessions. It states Lir's needs as behaviour and numbers. Lir's storage layout
(3 x 256 per-shard head spaces, approved 2026-11-04) is negotiable; the
behaviour is not. Where it says what a library has today, it is read from
Dagda `0.1.3` and Dannan `0.5.6` (headers, capability reports and plans), not
built or measured by Lir; corrections are welcome.

**What became of it (added 2026-11-19).** This file is the record of Lir's
side on 2026-11-13 and is kept as written; its "today" is that day. Tom ruled
on the designs that followed (`ACTIVE.md`, "Decisions", 2026-11-13), and
Lir's design on the ruled nouns is `2026-11-13-step5-design.md`. In short:
- N1: the small-message bench is still owed by Dannan.
- N2: adverts are aggregated; it lands with Dagda's slice 2.
- N3: the partition reading came in Dannan 0.7.0, and Lir uses it.
- N4: the ABI promise holds from Dannan 0.6.0. A hosted system pins one
  Dagda tag and takes its Dannan minor.
- G1 to G9: the log is a Dagda noun (inflight, session queues, history);
  retained is a register-map space with expiry per key; head spaces take
  `replicas(N, scheme, capability)` and a k per space; leases sit on head
  spaces; node-local spaces exist. Contract v2 freezes at slice 4.
- The PUBACK budget (answer 3): p99 under 50 ms over the whole path (Tom).
- R1 to R11 were all taken into Dagda's design before Tom ruled. R7 no
  longer applies to the inflight log, since Dagda groups a tick's appends.
- R2's bound is a stated limit: a split longer than 10 minutes can deliver
  a QoS 2 message twice.

"Tom's answers", cited below as in `ACTIVE.md`, are now in `COMPLETED.md`
under "Archived from ACTIVE.md, 2026-11-14". "plan N" is line N of
`2026-11-03-lir-plan.md`.

## What Lir is, in the terms that load the libraries

- An MQTT 3.1, 3.1.1 and 5.0 broker. It is clustered and multimaster: any node
  accepts any client operation, and both sides of a split serve and merge
  after.
- Load (Tom, 2026-11-04): up to about 10,000 publishes a second across the
  cluster, mostly under 1 KiB, with a 64 KiB inline limit. The QoS mix is
  unknown; plan for all of it being QoS 1/2.
- QoS 0 is Ephemeral and waits on nothing. QoS 1/2 is acknowledged (PUBACK or
  PUBREC) only once the message is Replicated (k = 2 by default). The latency
  target is a batch tick of 10 to 20 ms plus one replication round.
- Fan-out is direct, edge to edge on Dannan's channels. Wildcard indexes are
  soft state in Lir's memory, rebuilt from announcements. Neither is Dagda's.

## Answers to the Dagda session's questions (2026-11-13)

("stack", "Lir on Dagda", lines 395-463). The design gives no sizes, so the
figures below are the Lir session's **estimates**, marked as such. Where a

### 1. Sizes and rates, per kind of state

Load: 10,000 publishes a second cluster-wide (Tom's answers 4), so about 39 a
second per shard at S = 256 (plan 59). Shards hold R = 3 holders (plan 62).

| State | Entry | Entries per shard | Writes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inflight log (plan 108) | One envelope (plan 72-83): a header of about 60 bytes, the topic (about 50), MQTT 5 properties and the payload inline up to 64 KiB, mostly under 1 KiB; plus the set of edges still owed. Estimate: 0.3 to 1.5 KiB. | Residence is from acceptance until every target edge acks, one fan-out round, so in steady state about 2 to 4 entries (39/s x 50-100 ms). Worst case, an edge stays owed until Dannan's roster drops it (sections 2: "Lir adds no failure timer"): 39/s x Dannan's absence window. | Per message: one add, then one removal per target edge's ack (plan 126: one reply acks a batch). The plan (rev. 7, plan 41, 110-112) had a session head per client id and a
wildcard head per edge. The layout Tom approved on 2026-11-04 (Tom's answers 6)
replaced these with per-shard spaces for retained, inflight and sessions and the
wildcard index in memory. The layout is negotiable; the behaviour is not.

### 2. One writer per shard on the hot path

Yes in steady state, and the design already forwards to it. An edge sends a
publish to the shard's primary, the holder with the lease `lir/shard/<n>` (plan
40, 55, 63, 126-131), and the primary writes the inflight log and the retained
set. So any-node acceptance is forwarded and the hot path has one writer. Two
exceptions must be carried, not prevented:
- Two holders may briefly both hold the lease. Lir is idempotent under it, and
  receivers fence the lower epoch (plan 40, 114).
- In a split each side takes the lease and serves: both sides write, and the
  heads merge after (plan 13, 103; stack "Decided" 19, multimaster).

So heads may be optimised for one writer, with divergence the rare path.

### 3. The PUBACK latency budget

An aim, not a ruling. The design says only that PUBACK waits for the inflight
head's ticket to reach `write_copies` (plan 43, 131). The 10-20 ms batch is the
Lir session's estimate in the stack document. Tom has set no number; the Lir
session will ask him.

### 4. What a split must guarantee for inflight state

- No loss, duplicates allowed. The inflight merge is the union of entries, and
  per entry the intersection of edges still owed: an edge acked on either side
  has it (plan 108). Sessions likewise: the higher takeover counter wins, and
  filters and inflight sets are unioned (plan 110; sections 2).
- QoS 2 stays exactly once per session within the dedup window, by the dot,
  across the merge (plan 132-134; window 10 minutes, sections 2).
- A side that cannot reach k replicas, per node: degrade, the default (write
  Persisted, PUBACK, count it under-replicated, re-replicate on heal), or
  refuse with 0x97 after a deadline (sections 2).
- Ordering per (topic, origin) holds by seq; across origins it may interleave
  differently over an epoch change (plan 85-90).

Retained differs: per topic the greater dot wins, with fenced entries losing
ties (plan 107, 114). Tom's later ruling orders it by Lamport counter, then node
id (Tom's answers 3).

## Lir's review of Dagda's draft (2026-11-13)

Of `../dagda/TODO/2026-11-13-three-systems-design.md`. Lir backs the direction: a log
noun for the hot path, replicas(N, scheme, capability) with per-space k, retained as a
register map, leases on head spaces, node-local spaces, generations on channels, and the
measurement first. The points below are where the draft does not yet carry Lir's
semantics. The first five are correctness; the rest are fit and order.

**The log (Dagda's question 1): close, but not yet enough.**

- R1. An entry's required acks. "An entry goes when every ack it needs is present" (draft
  102-103) does not say who names what it needs. Lir appends for durability before fan-out
  (PUBACK waits on Replicated, plan 131), and the target set changes after: an edge leaves
  the roster, or a shared subscription moves to the next edge after `deliver_timeout_ms`
  (plan 154). Needed: the owner can retire an entry by dot with a final ack ("done"), and
  that is merged like any ack. Alternatively, a targets entry appended after the entry, plus
  a waiver for an edge that left. Lir prefers the first: Dagda never needs to know what an
  edge is.
- R2. No resurrection across a split. When one side removes an entry and its acks (whole
  segments deleted, draft 109-110), the other side's older copy comes back on heal under a
  union merge, and is delivered again. QoS 1 allows that. QoS 2 does not, once the
  subscriber edge's dedup window (10 minutes, sections 2) has passed. Needed: the log keeps
  removed dots, as tombstones, for an owner-named window, and a merge never re-admits a dot
  in that set. Also, a stated bound: a split longer than the window may deliver a QoS 2
  message twice. That bound goes to Tom as a documented limit.
- R3. Acks that carry state. A session's to-client QoS 2 flow is a small state machine per
  message (PUBLISH sent with a packet id, PUBREC received, PUBREL sent, PUBCOMP), not one
  ack. Needed: an ack carries a few owner bytes (packet id, stage), and a read returns live
  entries with their acks. Lir orders the stages so their union is still correct (the
  highest stage wins).
- R4. Order on read. MQTT requires order per (topic, origin) for QoS 1 and 2 (plan 87).
  Needed: a read within a stream returns, for each origin, its entries in seq order,
  including after a merge.
- R5. A scan for a new primary. After a lease moves, the new holder must resume fan-out of
  everything still owed in the shard, across all streams. Needed: a paged read of every live
  entry in a log, not only one stream.
- R6. Bounds per stream. An offline session's queue is capped per session (10,000 messages,
  sections 4), and the oldest QoS 0 goes first. Needed: a count bound per stream key, or the
  live count per stream so Lir can enforce it.
- R7. One batch, not two. Lir already batches per edge queue (plan 126, `batch_delay_ms`).
  A second Dagda tick on top would double the latency. Needed: an append of a batch is
  committed at once, with the log's tick configurable down to zero for an owner that batches
  itself.
- R8. Payloads over 64 KiB. Log entries are inline-only (draft 98). Lir's larger payloads
  are objects with claims (plan 39). Lir will claim them itself and release on the final ack
  with the instant delete. This needs nothing new; it's said so the design assumes it.

**Retained as a register map (Dagda's question 2): enough for the value, not for the read.**

- R9. Expiry per entry. An MQTT 5 retained message carries a message expiry. Needed: an
  expiry per key in the register map, or Lir clears it itself with a commit at expiry (more
  commits). Lir prefers expiry in the map.
- R10. The wildcard read. A SUBSCRIBE with a wildcard must return every retained topic it
  matches. With topics hashed to 256 shards (plan 61), that is every shard. A node holds
  only the shards it replicates, so a wildcard subscribe would make up to 256 forwarded,
  paged reads. Options: (a) Lir keeps an in-memory index of retained topics on every node,
  fed by a cheap cluster-wide notice of keys changed (D4's `changed`, for spaces this node
  does not hold); or (b) a forwarded paged scan per space, used at subscribe time. Lir
  prefers (a); Dagda to say which it can serve.
- R11. Values above 64 KiB as objects: the same as R8.

**Session state, not queues.** The draft gives session queues to logs but does not say
where a session's own state goes: filters, will, expiry, receive maximum, holder edge and
takeover counter (plan 110). Lir reads it as a replicas(3) head space per shard, with Lir's
merge (higher takeover counter wins; equal counters, union). Its rate is low: connect,
subscribe and take-over only. Dagda to confirm.

**Space and log count.** Lir's step 5, as drafted: per shard, an inflight log, a session
queue log, a retained space, a session-state space and a lease space. That is about 1,280
spaces and logs at S = 256, each node holding about 3/N of them, and each with a notify
channel. Needed from the measurement: memory, file handles and channels per space and per
log on a node, at 256 and at 1,280. If it does not fit, Lir will cut the count: leases in
one space per shard with the session state, or S = 64.

**The order (Dagda's question 3).** Lir's step 5 needs slices 1 to 4 (2 + 3 + 4 + 3 + 5 =
17 sessions with slice 0). Contract v2 is frozen only at slice 5, after the domain catalogue,
which Lir does not need, so Lir could pin only after about 21 sessions. Asks:
- Freeze v2 when slice 4 lands, and add the catalogue in a later minor (v2 additive). Or
  freeze each noun as its slice lands, with additive versions after.
- Put the log's prototype in slice 0's measurement with Lir's real pattern: a batch per
  append, acks per edge, a final retire, and the 1,280-space count. The log is the riskiest
  piece and the one most likely to change a format.
- Lir builds step 5 against each slice's tag as it lands, unfrozen, at Lir's own risk. Lir
  ships nothing until v2 is frozen.

## What Lir proposes for the discussion

1. Dannan and Dagda each say how they would serve Macha, Lugh and Lir as
   first-class users: the nouns, the limits (spaces, adverts, rates) and the
   versioning. Then they say what that changes in Macha's port order. Tom
   rules.
2. G6 and N1 are measured early, before Macha's later stage builds heads'
   formats in, because a wrong answer changes formats.
3. The smallest first slice that would let Lir start step 5 against a pinned
   tag, as Lir sees it: G4, G5, G7 and a measured G6, on a Dannan that answers
   N2 and N4. The libraries may cut it differently.
4. Lugh's needs (stack document: node-local spaces, no rollback, the scope and
   secrets domains, per-origin counters) and Macha's carry the same weight in
   that design.

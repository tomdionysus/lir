## Purpose and scope

Lir is the event system of the sovereign-cloud family: a clustered publish/subscribe bus that is a Dannan hosted system and presents itself to clients as one MQTT broker. Connect to any Lir node, publish or subscribe anywhere, reconnect anywhere; the cluster behaves as a single broker except where a partition makes that impossible, and then it degrades to two brokers that merge rather than to an outage.

Lir sits beside the KV store, the filesystem and the compute platform, not under or over them. The only thing it shares with them is Dannan. It does not read their state and they do not read its; they talk to it, if at all, by publishing on a local channel.

**In scope**

- MQTT 5.0 and 3.1.1 on every Lir node: QoS 0/1/2, retained messages, wills, wildcards, shared subscriptions, persistent sessions that follow a client between nodes.
- Survival of node loss and partition with no coordinator, using Dannan leases, heads and claims.
- Substrate state as retained system topics, so dashboards and automation need no other feed.
- An HTTP publish and SSE subscribe endpoint on the same bus, for browsers and curl.

**Out of scope**

- A durable log or replay. Lir keeps retained messages, subscriptions and in-flight QoS 1/2 state, nothing else. A message with no subscriber is gone.
- Bridging to external brokers. A separate hosted system can do that later.
- Total order across topics, or across publishers on one topic across a failover.
- Public multi-tenant service. Lir runs inside Dannan's trust and scale envelope: a soft cluster of trusted nodes, tens to low hundreds.

## What Lir takes from Dannan

Lir is written entirely against the Dannan API at seam 8. It introduces no cluster-wide state of its own: everything it keeps is a Head, an Object or a Claim; everything it sends is on a Channel; everything it coordinates is a Lease. The table is the whole contract.

| Dannan noun | How Lir uses it |
|---|---|
| **Node** | Lir runs only on nodes advertising the `lir` capability. Placement of shard holders is over those nodes; bare Dannan nodes contribute roster, reach and durability for Lir's objects, nothing else. |
| **Object** | Payloads larger than the inline limit are stored as immutable Objects; the envelope carries the content hash. Nothing else Lir keeps is an Object. |
| **Claim** | A shard holder holds a Claim dot on every out-of-line payload that is retained or in flight, and releases it on expiry or final ack. GC never has to know what a message is. |
| **Lease** | Primary holdership of a topic shard is `acquire("lir/shard/<n>", scope)` -> `(holder, epoch)`. Every delivery carries the epoch; a stale holder is fenced by receivers, never blocked. Lir assumes two holders may briefly coexist and is idempotent under it. |
| **Head** | Three head families, all owned and merged by Lir: the retained set per shard, the wildcard subscription index (one head per edge), and persistent MQTT sessions (one head per client id). Dannan replicates and reports divergence; Lir supplies the merge. |
| **Channel** | Three channels opened at start with their contracts declared: `lir.notify` (at-most-once, CONTROL) for QoS 0 and subscription refresh; `lir.seq` (at-least-once, per-origin sequence) for QoS 1/2, subscribe, ack and lease traffic; `lir.bulk` (DATA, backpressure) for out-of-line payload transfer. |
| **Ticket** | A QoS 1/2 PUBACK is sent when the Ticket for the shard head generation that includes the message reaches the write floor (`write_copies`). Accept-locally and durable are visibly separate, as Dannan intends. |
| **Budget** | Lir declares its unit of work (one max-size envelope batch per edge queue) and its memory, I/O and cache budget per work class. Dannan refuses to start it on a node whose budget is smaller. |
| **Fault** | Lir is supervised. Faults go to the sink; restart is policy; a node that cannot recover its heads refuses to serve Lir rather than adopting empty state. |

Rules Lir inherits and does not relitigate: no global clock (per-origin sequence and dots only); no consensus or leader (a holder is a lease, not an election); exactly-once does not exist in the substrate; hosted systems never call each other; identity is Lugh's; reachability (direct, punched, relayed) is resolved below Lir and invisible to it.

## Node roles and topology

A Lir node is three things at once: an **edge** (MQTT listener, client sessions, Lugh scope checks), a **shard holder** (primary or replica for the topic shards HRW assigns it), and a **subscriber edge** (delivering to its own clients). A publish makes at most two hops, and neither is a lookup: every node derives the holders for a shard from the roster it already has.

(The doc embeds a diagram here: "publish path: edge, shard holder, edge; Dannan underneath".)

The primary for a shard is whichever holder currently has the Lease. In the steady state that is the top-ranked HRW node, because it is the one that asked first; after a failure it is whichever replica took the lease after the absence window. Receivers do not care which: they accept the highest epoch they have seen and fence the rest.

## Topics, shards and ownership

Topics are not placed individually. A topic maps to one of `S` shards (default 256), and shards are what get holders and leases. This bounds the number of leases Dannan tracks for Lir to `S`, and makes a roster change move about `R/N` of shards rather than a re-placement of every topic.

- `shard(topic) = sha256("lir:" || prefix(topic, L)) mod S`, where `L = ownership_prefix_levels` (default 0, the full topic). With `L = 2`, `sensors/room1/temp` and `sensors/room1/hum` share a shard, so a filter like `sensors/room1/+` can be placed with that shard instead of going into the global wildcard index.
- Holders for shard `n` are the top `R` (default 3) lir-capable nodes by HRW over `sha256(n || nodeId)`, preferring distinct failure domains, using Dannan's placement call rather than a Lir copy of it.
- The primary is the holder with the Lease `lir/shard/<n>`. Lease scope is the Lir principal; the epoch is carried on every delivery and every head write for that shard.
- A node joining or leaving changes holder sets for the shards it ranks into or out of. The affected primaries push head state to new replicas; edges re-announce exact subscriptions for the moved shards. Nothing else moves.

Reserved prefixes: `$dannan/` for substrate state and `$lir/` for Lir's own state, both subscribe-only; `$share/<group>/` for shared subscriptions; `$macha/`, `$lugh/` and other hosted-system prefixes belong to those systems and are published by them over `lir.local`.

## Envelope and ordering

An event is an immutable envelope identified by `(origin, seq)`: the publishing edge's NodeId and its persisted per-edge counter. That is the Dannan dot, and it is the only identity Lir ever orders or dedups on.

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `topic` | string | Full MQTT topic, UTF-8, validated at the edge |
| `shard` | u16 | Computed at the edge; holders check, never recompute |
| `origin` | NodeId | Publishing edge |
| `seq` | u64 | Per-origin, persisted; the dot |
| `epoch` | u64 | Shard lease epoch at the primary that accepted it (set by the holder, not the edge) |
| `ts` | u64 | Origin wall-clock ms; passed to clients, never used for ordering or merge |
| `qos`, `retain` | u8, bool | As the client sent them |
| `expiry` | u32 | MQTT 5 message expiry seconds, 0 = none |
| `props` | bytes | MQTT 5 properties passed through opaquely |
| `payload` | bytes or ObjectId | Inline up to `inline_payload_max` (64 KiB); otherwise a Dannan Object with a Claim held by the primary |

**Ordering**

- Per `(topic, origin)`: `seq` order, for QoS 1 and 2. This is the MQTT ordering guarantee and it holds across failover because `seq` is the origin's, not the holder's.
- Across origins on one topic: primary arrival order under one epoch. Across an epoch change the new primary may interleave differently. Lir documents this and does not pretend otherwise.
- Across topics: none. Correlation is the client's, in `props`.
- QoS 0 may reorder or drop under loss; it is `lir.notify` and promises what that channel promises.

## Heads and their merge functions

Lir has exactly three kinds of mutable state and each is a Dannan Head with a Lir-supplied merge. Dannan stores, replicates to the holders, reports divergence and calls `merge(base?, a, b)`; it never decides. Every merge below is commutative, associative and idempotent, so the order heads arrive in after a partition does not matter.

| Head | One per | Contents | Merge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Retained set** | shard | map `topic -> envelope` plus a tombstone map `topic -> dot` for retained messages cleared by an empty publish | per topic, the entry with the greater dot wins; dots are compared by `(seq, origin)` per origin and are incomparable across origins, so across origins the tie-break is lowest `origin` byte-wise. Tombstones compete on the same rule and win ties. Never `ts`. |
| **Inflight log** | shard | QoS 1/2 envelopes accepted but not yet acked by every target edge, keyed by dot, with the set of edges still owed | union of entries; per entry, intersection of edges still owed (an edge acked on either side has it) |
| **Wildcard index** | edge | that edge's wildcard filters with session ids, qos and the edge's membership incarnation | newest incarnation wins outright; same incarnation, union of filters. The whole set is small and is replaced, not diffed. |
| **Session** | client id | filters, inflight to-client and from-client QoS 1/2 state, will, expiry, receive maximum, holder edge, takeover counter | highest takeover counter wins; equal counters, union of filters and of inflight sets (a duplicate delivery is the correct outcome of a session that split) |

The retained set and inflight log are replicated to the shard's holders; the wildcard index to every lir-capable node (it is the one piece of state every node needs); a session to its holder edge plus two standby holders placed by HRW on `sha256("lir-sess:" || clientId)`.

Head writes carry the shard lease epoch. A holder that receives a head write under an epoch lower than one it has already accepted stores it (Dannan guarantees nothing is overwritten) but marks it fenced, and the merge treats fenced entries as having lost any tie. This is what keeps a stale primary from resurrecting a cleared retained message.

## Channels and delivery contracts

Lir opens three Dannan channels at start and never sends outside them. A message's MQTT QoS picks the channel, so the guarantee a client gets is exactly the one the substrate makes on that channel; Lir adds idempotence on top for QoS 2 and nothing else.

| Channel | Dannan contract | Lane / class | Carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lir.notify` | at-most-once | CONTROL, fast-control class | QoS 0 publish and deliver; subscription refresh; wildcard index gossip |
| `lir.seq` | at-least-once, per-origin sequence | CONTROL, control class | QoS 1/2 publish and deliver; subscribe, unsubscribe, ack; session take; holder sync; lease epoch announcements |
| `lir.bulk` | bulk with backpressure | DATA, loader class | out-of-line payload Objects; full head transfer to a new holder |

Messages on `lir.seq` are the verbs `publish`, `deliver`, `ack`, `subscribe`, `unsubscribe`, `session_take`, `holder_sync`, each a small struct in the Dannan codec; `publish` and `deliver` carry a vector of envelopes (up to `batch_max`, coalesced for `batch_delay_ms`) and one reply acks the batch.

| Client QoS | Edge to holder | Holder durability | Holder to edge | Edge to client | Net guarantee |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `lir.notify`, primary only | none | `lir.notify` | PUBLISH | at most once |
| 1 | `lir.seq`; PUBACK when the inflight-log head Ticket reaches `write_copies` | inflight log head, replicated to holders | `lir.seq`, retried until the edge acks or leaves the roster | PUBLISH with packet id, retried until PUBACK | at least once |
| 2 | as QoS 1; primary dedups on the dot for `dedup_window` | as QoS 1 plus dedup table | as QoS 1; subscriber edge dedups on the dot per session | full PUBREC/PUBREL/PUBCOMP, inflight in the session head | exactly once per session within the window |

Exactly-once is Lir's idempotence, not a substrate promise, and it is bounded by `dedup_window` (default 10 minutes). A client that needs more carries its own idempotency key in `props`.

**Backpressure.** The per-edge outbound queue at a holder is bounded by Lir's declared Budget. Past it, QoS 0 is dropped oldest-first and counted; QoS 1/2 publishes are refused to the publisher with MQTT 5 `0x97 Quota exceeded`, so the pressure lands on the producer. Receive Maximum applies per client session on the way out.

## Subscriptions and fan-out

A subscription is `(filter, edge, session, qos, options, incarnation)`. Where it lives depends on whether its wildcards fall inside the shard's ownership prefix.

- **Shard-local filters** (no wildcard, or wildcards only beyond `ownership_prefix_levels`) go to the shard holders by `subscribe` on `lir.seq`. The primary keeps a `topicKey -> set<(edge, session, qos)>` table and a small per-shard trie for the local wildcards. Replicas receive the same call so a lease change loses nothing.
- **Global wildcard filters** (`sensors/#`, `+/status`, `#`) go into the edge's wildcard-index Head, replicated to every lir-capable node. Each node merges all edges' heads into one trie keyed on `/` segments with `+` and `#` nodes.

**Fan-out** happens once, at the primary: it looks up the exact set, walks the shard-local trie, walks the global trie, unions the edges, and sends one `deliver` per edge. Session ids are not sent; the edge matches the topic against its own sessions' filters with the same trie code and writes a PUBLISH per matching session. Holders know edges; only edges know clients.

**Lifecycle**

1. SUBSCRIBE at edge E: record in the session head; `subscribe` to holders or update E's wildcard head; the reply (or a `retained` query for wildcards) carries retained matches, delivered to the client first.
2. Roster change: E recomputes holders for every shard it has subscriptions in and re-announces to any that moved. Holders drop entries whose edge left the roster, by incarnation, on the membership diff.
3. Backstop: E re-announces its full shard-local set every `resync_ms` (default 30 s). Holders treat this as idempotent set union with incarnation-based expiry.
4. UNSUBSCRIBE or session end: `unsubscribe`, and the wildcard head is rewritten. A persistent session that disconnects keeps its subscriptions until its expiry.

**Shared subscriptions** (`$share/<group>/<filter>`): the primary holds one logical subscriber per group and round-robins deliveries across the group's edges; each edge round-robins across its sessions in the group. A QoS 1 delivery unacked after `deliver_timeout_ms` goes to the next edge in the group.
